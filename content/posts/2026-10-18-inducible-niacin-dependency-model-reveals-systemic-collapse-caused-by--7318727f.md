A new mouse model of chronic NAD depletion drives rapid tissue decay and senescence, proving reversible through dietary repletion.

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide acts as a fundamental metabolic cofactor driving cellular energy production, epigenetic regulation, and DNA repair. To isolate the systemic consequences of its chronic decline, researchers engineered acquired niacin dependency mice, forcing cellular synthesis to rely entirely on dietary intake. When deprived of niacin, these animals experienced widespread tissue NAD depletion accompanied by severe physical deterioration, including kyphosis, cataracts, muscle and fat atrophy, fur browning, and shortened lifespan. At the cellular level, this metabolic starvation triggered persistent tissue inflammation and senescence, driving broad transcriptomic remodeling that mapped only partially onto natural chronological aging. Clearing senescent cells with pharmacological treatments partially rescued physical function without altering tissue NAD pools, demonstrating that the structural decay stemmed directly from metabolic failure rather than downstream senescence alone.

Crucially, restoring dietary niacin reversed the biochemical, phenotypic, and transcriptional collapse, proving that replenishing the cofactor rescues systemic tissue architecture. However, because this evidence derives entirely from an engineered murine model, whether human tissues undergo identical cascades during chronic depletion remains unproven. The findings establish that sustained metabolic cofactor scarcity operates as an independent driver of multi-organ decay, distinct from standard aging pathways, while offering a clear boundary between primary metabolic failure and secondary cellular damage.

## Key takeaways

- Genetically modified mice dependent on dietary niacin allowed researchers to track the exact systemic toll of chronic NAD deprivation.
- Depleting the metabolic cofactor drove rapid tissue decay, widespread cellular senescence, metabolic remodeling, and shortened lifespan.
- Restoring dietary niacin reversed the biochemical, physical, and transcriptional abnormalities, proving that metabolic rescue restores systemic tissue function.
