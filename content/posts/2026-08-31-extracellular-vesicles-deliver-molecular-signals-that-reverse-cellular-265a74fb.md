Tiny cellular mailbags dictate whether tissues regenerate or decay, shifting mitochondrial function and inflammation across organ systems depending on their cellular origin.

A comprehensive narrative review of literature across the Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed databases reveals that extracellular vesicles, particularly exosomes, act as master regulators of cell-to-cell communication during biological aging. When tissues experience genomic instability, mitochondrial decay, and chronic inflammation, these microscopic lipid-bilayer packages shuttle proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids between neighboring and distant cells. The review examines preclinical and clinical studies tracking how vesicles shed by young or stem cells reprogram aged environments. These young vesicles mitigate cellular senescence, restart stalled mitochondrial respiration, and clear damaged organelles by re-engaging autophagic and mitophagic machinery across immune, musculoskeletal, cardiovascular, and nervous systems.

Conversely, the review highlights a striking biological duality where vesicles shed by senescent or diseased cells actively propagate pro-aging phenotypes, accelerating tissue fibrosis and metabolic breakdown. While this cross-system signaling positions extracellular vesicles as powerful therapeutic targets capable of addressing multiple hallmarks of aging simultaneously, moving these interventions from animal models to human medicine remains heavily constrained. The narrative synthesis underscores that current investigative efforts face significant hurdles, including high heterogeneity among vesicle subpopulations, inconsistent laboratory isolation protocols, and wide variations in the active biological payloads delivered by these cellular parcels.

## Key takeaways

- Extracellular vesicles act as biological couriers, shuttling molecular payloads between cells to dictate tissue fate during aging.
- Vesicles derived from young stem cells restore mitochondrial activity, clear damaged proteins, and suppress chronic tissue inflammation.
- Vesicles originating from aged or diseased cells accelerate structural decay by spreading fibrotic and inflammatory signals.
