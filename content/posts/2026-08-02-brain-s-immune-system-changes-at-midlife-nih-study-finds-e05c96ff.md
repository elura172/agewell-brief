## Brain's Immune System Changes at Midlife, NIH Study Finds

New research suggests a major shift in brain immunity around middle age, which may be linked to cognitive decline and dementia risk.

### Three things to know

- NIH-supported findings reveal a new aspect of human brain aging.
- Midlife immune changes may contribute to age-related cognitive decline.
- This discovery could help understand dementia risk in older adults.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The NIH study identifies a major shift in brain immunity around midlife, likely involving changes in microglial activation, cytokine profiles, and meningeal immune cell composition. This connects to Dénes's work on age-related meningeal immune changes and Schwartz's findings on the protective role of meningeal T cells, suggesting that midlife immune alterations may disrupt neuroimmune homeostasis, leading to chronic neuroinflammation and increased dementia risk.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional lifestyles, such as those with regular physical activity, intermittent fasting, and diverse plant-based diets, are known to modulate systemic inflammation and support immune function. These practices may help maintain a balanced brain immune environment into midlife, potentially mitigating the adverse effects of the midlife immune shift observed in the study, thereby preserving cognitive health.
