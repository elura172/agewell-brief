The Higher Flame guide is built around metabolic flexibility: the ability to move between carbohydrate and fat as fuel. It combines a time-limited eating window with plant compounds that the program describes as signals for the body’s own antioxidant systems.

## Why this matters

Fasting is no longer a fringe topic. It is studied in relation to body weight, glucose regulation, sleep, and circadian timing. Results vary, and the same schedule can feel manageable for one person and destabilizing for another.

The useful question is not whether fasting is universally good. It is whether a particular pattern fits a person’s health, medication, work, sleep, and relationship with food.

## What the framework emphasizes

The guide pairs an 18:6 eating window with broccoli sprouts, sulforaphane, resveratrol, curcumin, and NAC. Its conceptual centre is hormesis: a small stress signal that may prompt adaptive responses. It also emphasizes that growing broccoli sprouts can be an inexpensive food-based experiment.

Those ideas can be explored without assuming that a concentrated supplement reproduces the effect of a food, or that a short intensive produces a permanent metabolic change.

## How to interpret it

Claims about TOR, ketones, brain fuel, and NRF2 are grounded in real areas of biology, but the popular language can outrun the evidence. Ketones are not automatically “twenty times more powerful” in a way that translates into a universal cognitive benefit, and fasting is not appropriate for everyone.

People with diabetes, a history of eating disorders, frailty, pregnancy, or medication schedules affected by food should seek clinical guidance before changing meal timing. Symptoms such as fainting, confusion, or persistent weakness are reasons to stop and seek care.

## Practical next steps

Start with the least dramatic version: consistent meal timing, fewer ultra-processed snacks, adequate protein and fibre, and a sleep schedule that supports appetite regulation. If you experiment with a shorter eating window, keep it gradual and track energy, sleep, mood, and medication effects.

- Begin gently and review medication or health risks first.
