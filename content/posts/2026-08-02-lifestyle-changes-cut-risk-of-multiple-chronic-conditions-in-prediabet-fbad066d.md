## Lifestyle Changes Cut Risk of Multiple Chronic Conditions in Prediabetes

A long-term NIH trial found lifestyle intervention reduced risk of developing several chronic diseases, while metformin showed no difference from placebo.

### Three things to know

- Lifestyle intervention lowered risk of multiple chronic conditions in adults with prediabetes.
- Long-term NIH-supported trial compared lifestyle changes, metformin, and placebo.
- Metformin showed no difference from placebo in reducing risk of these conditions.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The DPP lifestyle intervention (diet + 150 min/week physical activity) targets the shared root cause of multimorbidity: insulin resistance and its downstream effects on endothelial function, lipid metabolism, and inflammation. Weight loss reduces visceral adiposity (Vague's android fat), which lowers free fatty acid flux and ectopic lipid deposition, improving insulin sensitivity (Reaven's syndrome X). This restores insulin-mediated glucose uptake, reduces hyperinsulinemia, and mitigates the chronic low-grade inflammation that drives cardiovascular disease, hypertension, and dyslipidemia—explaining the reduced incidence of multiple chronic conditions. Metformin, while improving hepatic insulin sensitivity, does not address the broader inflammatory and adiposity-driven pathways as effectively, consistent with its null effect on multimorbidity in this trial.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional hunter-gatherer and agrarian lifestyles inherently incorporated intermittent physical activity and periodic caloric scarcity, which maintained insulin sensitivity and low visceral fat stores. The DPP's lifestyle arm mimics these ancestral patterns—regular movement and modest energy restriction—whereas modern sedentary, energy-dense diets promote chronic hyperinsulinemia and ectopic fat accumulation. This evolutionary mismatch explains why lifestyle intervention, not metformin, restores the metabolic flexibility that ancestral humans possessed, thereby preventing the cluster of chronic diseases that arise from insulin resistance.
