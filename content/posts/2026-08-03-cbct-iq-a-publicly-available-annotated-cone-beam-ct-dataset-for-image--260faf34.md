A new annotated dataset of 1,764 cone-beam CT slices enables reproducible research on quantitative image quality assessment, balancing diagnostic accuracy with radiation dose.



## Three things to remember

- 1,764 annotated CBCT slices with expert scores for overall and ROI quality.
- 26 full- and no-reference IQA measures benchmarked against expert annotations.
- Exploratory ranking distinguishes subtle image quality differences for future research.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The CBCT-IQ dataset directly addresses the trade-off between image quality and radiation dose, a core concern in CT imaging since Hounsfield's era. By providing expert-annotated quality scores and benchmarking 26 IQA measures, it enables reproducible research on optimizing acquisition parameters to minimize dose while maintaining diagnostic accuracy. This connects to Kalender's work by focusing on CBCT-specific artifacts and quality assessment, validating the need for dedicated quality metrics in this modality.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional diagnostic practices, such as palpation and observation, relied on qualitative assessment of physical signs without radiation exposure. The modern push for quantitative image quality assessment with minimal dose echoes the ancestral principle of 'first, do no harm'—using the least invasive method to obtain necessary information. The dataset's emphasis on balancing diagnostic accuracy with dose reduction aligns with this timeless medical ethic.
