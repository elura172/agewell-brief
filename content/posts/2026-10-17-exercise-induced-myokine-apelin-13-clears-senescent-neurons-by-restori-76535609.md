By engaging cellular recycling machinery, apelin signaling sweeps away inflammatory proteins in aging brains, preserving cognitive function in animal models.

Brain aging gathers momentum when neurons accumulate oxidative stress and inflammatory transcription factors that halt cellular recycling. Researchers tested whether apelin-13, an exercise-induced circulating protein, could counter this cellular stagnation in human neuroblastoma cells, induced pluripotent stem cell-derived neurons, and a D-galactose-induced mouse model of accelerated aging. Rather than acting as a direct chemical sponge to scavenge reactive oxygen species, apelin-13 binds to the G protein-coupled receptor APJ to trigger an intracellular cascade. This signal activates protein kinase C and calcium-calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II, which in turn phosphorylates and activates AMP-activated protein kinase. The resulting suppression of mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1 releases transcription factor EB from sequestration, allowing it to migrate into the nucleus, rebuild lysosomal networks, and restart stalled autophagic flux.

Restoring this cellular cleanup pathway drives the physical destruction of GATA4, a transcription factor that accumulates during stress to fan the flames of paracrine senescence by churning out pro-inflammatory cytokines. By clearing GATA4 through restored autophagy, apelin signaling halts the inflammatory ripple effect across neural tissue, while administering the apelin receptor agonist ML233 preserves cognitive performance in aging mice. However, because these mechanistic insights derive entirely from targeted cellular cultures and chemically induced animal models rather than human clinical trials, the translation of these pathways to human neurodegeneration remains an open question.

## Key takeaways

- Apelin-13 binds the APJ receptor to activate PKC and CaMKII, which phosphorylate AMPK and suppress mTORC1 activity.
- Inhibiting mTORC1 frees transcription factor EB to enter the nucleus, restoring lysosomal function and stalled autophagic flux.
- Restarted neuronal autophagy clears GATA4 proteins, stopping the spread of pro-inflammatory senescence signals in aging brain tissue.
