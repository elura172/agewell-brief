A large UK study finds that MRI measures of the choroid plexus—a structure that makes cerebrospinal fluid—track with later dementia risk. The finding is a signal, not a test.

## Three things to remember

- 45,306 UK Biobank participants were followed over time.
- Larger choroid plexus and lower signal intensity raised risk.
- This is an association, not a diagnostic tool.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The choroid plexus is the primary source of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), which circulates and clears metabolic waste from the brain. Its function is tightly coupled to glymphatic clearance, a process dependent on aquaporin-4 channels and mitochondrial ATP production. MRI measures of choroid plexus volume and signal intensity likely capture changes in water content, vascular permeability, and cellular metabolism. These changes may precede cognitive decline by years, as the choroid plexus becomes less efficient at CSF production and waste clearance, leading to accumulation of amyloid-beta and tau proteins. This aligns with Warburg's concept of metabolic dysregulation and Popp's biophotonic coherence, as mitochondrial dysfunction would disrupt both energy production and coherent electromagnetic signaling, impairing neural communication and clearance.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional lifestyle practices such as intermittent fasting, cold exposure, and adequate sleep are known to enhance mitochondrial function and autophagy. These practices may support choroid plexus health by improving mitochondrial efficiency and glymphatic clearance. For example, fasting increases ketone bodies, which serve as an alternative fuel for mitochondria, potentially reducing oxidative stress in the choroid plexus. Cold exposure activates brown adipose tissue and may enhance cerebral blood flow, while deep sleep is critical for glymphatic clearance. These ancestral-aligned behaviors could mitigate the age-related decline in choroid plexus function, thereby reducing dementia risk, as suggested by the MRI findings.
