A sweeping review of over 350 studies hints that eating less protein could boost health and lifespan, but experts urge caution.




## Three things to remember

- Review of 350+ papers links protein restriction to slower aging.
- May improve metabolism and reduce cellular damage.
- Could extend lifespan in some cases, but human evidence is limited.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The review synthesizes evidence that protein restriction lowers mTORC1 activity, reduces IGF-1 signaling, and enhances autophagy and mitochondrial efficiency. These pathways are directly linked to reduced oxidative stress and DNA damage, which are hallmarks of aging. This mechanistic framework connects to Warburg's metabolic focus and Selye's stress adaptation: protein restriction is a metabolic stressor that reprograms energy utilization (favoring oxidative phosphorylation over glycolysis) and induces protective stress responses, thereby slowing aging at the cellular level.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional diets, such as those of the Okinawans (who historically consumed a plant-based, low-protein diet) and certain hunter-gatherer populations, often featured cyclical protein availability due to seasonal food scarcity. This intermittent protein restriction likely activated the same hormetic pathways (mTOR inhibition, autophagy) that the review identifies as beneficial. The physical mechanism is evolutionary: our ancestors' metabolism adapted to periods of low protein intake, and modern overconsumption disrupts these adaptive pathways, accelerating aging.
