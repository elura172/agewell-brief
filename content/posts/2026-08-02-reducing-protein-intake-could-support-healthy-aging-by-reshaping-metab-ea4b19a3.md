A sweeping review of over 350 studies hints that eating less protein could boost health and lifespan, but experts urge caution.




## Three things to remember

- Review of 350+ papers links protein restriction to slower aging.
- May improve metabolism and reduce cellular damage.
- Could extend lifespan in some cases, but human evidence is limited.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The modern review synthesizes 350+ studies showing that protein restriction activates nutrient-sensing pathways (e.g., AMPK, mTOR, and IIS) that downregulate protein synthesis and upregulate autophagy and stress resistance. This directly validates Partridge's earlier finding that reduced amino acids extend lifespan via TOR/IIS signaling, and extends Walford's caloric restriction paradigm by isolating protein as the key macronutrient. The review also highlights reduced cellular damage (e.g., oxidative stress and DNA damage) and improved metabolic flexibility, which are the same downstream effects observed in historical models, now confirmed across multiple species including humans.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional hunter-gatherer diets, such as those of the Hadza or !Kung, were often protein-scarce relative to modern diets, with protein intake fluctuating seasonally and typically lower than current Western levels. This intermittent protein scarcity likely activated the same nutrient-sensing pathways (mTOR inhibition, autophagy) that the review links to slower aging. Additionally, many traditional cultures practice periodic fasting or consume plant-based proteins with lower leucine content, which mimics protein restriction and may have provided ancestral protection against age-related metabolic decline.
