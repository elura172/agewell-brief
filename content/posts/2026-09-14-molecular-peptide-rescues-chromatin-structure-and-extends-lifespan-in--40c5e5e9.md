By stabilizing a crucial heterochromatin protein, a novel engineered peptide dampens inflammatory senescence pathways and prolongs survival in mice carrying the premature aging LMNA mutation.

Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndrome arises from a mutated LMNA gene that wreaks structural havoc on the cell nucleus, yet the downstream chromatin alterations remain poorly mapped. Researchers investigating these nuclear anomalies discovered a positive correlation between the architectural chromatin factor HMGA1 and heterochromatin protein HP1β in both naturally aging mice and the LmnaG609G/G609G genetic mouse model. Multi-omics analyses reveal that HP1β physically packs chromatin down around the gene encoding angiopoietin-like protein 2, reducing local DNA accessibility and silencing the secretion of senescence-associated secretory phenotype factors that drive tissue degeneration. Meanwhile, HMGA1 shields HP1β from cellular clearance by recruiting the de-ubiquitinating enzyme USP7 to snip off degradation tags.

Capitalizing on this physical binding geometry, the team designed a unique HMGA1-derived peptide that blocks HP1β degradation, successfully alleviating cellular senescence in progeria models and significantly extending the lifespan of LmnaG609G/G609G mice. While these findings map a precise biophysical axis for nuclear rejuvenation, the evidence remains strictly confined to rodent models and cultured cells, leaving human clinical translation and potential systemic side effects entirely untested.

## Key takeaways

- HMGA1 recruits the de-ubiquitinating enzyme USP7 to physically stabilize heterochromatin protein HP1β against cellular degradation pathways.
- HP1β compacts chromatin accessibility at the ANGPTL2 locus to suppress inflammatory senescence-associated secretory phenotype factors.
- A custom-designed HMGA1 peptide prevents HP1β breakdown, mitigating cellular senescence and extending lifespan in progeria mutant mice.
