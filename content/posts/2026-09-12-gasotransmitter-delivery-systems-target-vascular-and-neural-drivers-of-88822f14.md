Engineered nanocarriers and prodrugs deliver nitric oxide, carbon monoxide, and hydrogen sulfide directly to tissues, relaxing smooth muscle and repairing damaged nerves in early models.

Vascular and neural healthspan depends on the precise local management of reactive chemistry, a domain where endogenous gasotransmitters like nitric oxide, carbon monoxide, and hydrogen sulfide act as master regulators. When these signaling gases fail, smooth muscle tone stiffens, oxidative stress accumulates, and tissue scarring sets in. Recent review findings highlight how modern chemical engineering bypasses the volatility of these molecules through nanocarriers and targeted prodrugs. By packaging these gases into specialized delivery systems, researchers enhance local absorption and cellular uptake, allowing the molecules to relax vascular smooth muscle, protect fragile nerve endings, and suppress fibrotic remodeling in animal models of diabetes, aging, and nerve injury.

While early animal studies and initial clinical trials demonstrate promising functional recovery across challenging pathologies, translating these gas-delivery systems into human therapies requires overcoming strict pharmacological hurdles. Controlling delivery kinetics, establishing precise therapeutic dosages, and personalizing administration routes remain formidable technical challenges that current trial designs have not yet resolved. These innovations outline a mechanistic path toward restructuring vascular medicine, but the translation from benchtop nanocarriers to routine clinical application awaits rigorous validation in larger, controlled human cohorts.

## Key takeaways

- Nitric oxide, carbon monoxide, and hydrogen sulfide coordinate vascular tone, cellular protection, and tissue remodeling.
- Advanced nanocarriers and prodrug formulations overcome systemic gas volatility to target injured smooth muscle and nerves.
- Early animal models and initial trials demonstrate functional recovery across diabetes, aging, and nerve damage.
