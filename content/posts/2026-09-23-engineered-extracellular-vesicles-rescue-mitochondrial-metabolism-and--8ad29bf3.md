By delivering mitochondrial proteins and restoring arginine metabolism, engineered extracellular vesicles reverse macrophage senescence and speed healing in aged and diabetic mouse models.

Chronic wounds in aged tissue fail to heal largely because local macrophages enter a dysfunctional state of cellular senescence, trapping them in an inefficient metabolic loop dominated by glycolysis. To break this metabolic paralysis, researchers developed extracellular vesicles engineered with 4-octyl itaconate and enriched with natural mitochondrial proteins. When applied to aged and diabetic mouse models, these engineered vesicles accelerated skin tissue repair by suppressing cellular senescence and clearing cellular dysfunctions. At the molecular level, the treatment shifted cellular energy production away from raw glycolysis, instead driving the arginine-oxidative phosphorylation axis. This metabolic rescue operated through a targeted increase in argininosuccinate synthetase 1, an enzyme that allowed senescent macrophages to clear damaged mitochondria via restored mitophagy.

While these findings map a clear physical pathway for rejuvenating aged immune cells by rewiring their internal energy production, the current evidence remains strictly preclinical. Because the data derive exclusively from mouse models of cutaneous injury, the long-term safety, dosing parameters, and efficacy of these engineered vesicles in human clinical settings have yet to be established.

## Key takeaways

- Macrophages in chronic aged wounds suffer from abnormal senescence driven by disrupted arginine and oxidative phosphorylation metabolism.
- Engineered vesicles enriched with mitochondrial proteins lower glycolysis and boost the arginine-oxidative phosphorylation axis via argininosuccinate synthetase 1.
- Restoring mitochondrial clearance and energy production accelerates tissue repair in both aged and diabetic mouse skin models.
