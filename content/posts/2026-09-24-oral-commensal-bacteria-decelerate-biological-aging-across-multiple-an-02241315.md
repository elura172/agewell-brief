AI screening identifies an oral microbe that extends nematode lifespan and restores youthful metabolic profiles in aged mice.

Systemic aging leaves distinct physiological footprints across human tissues, yet the microbial residents of the mouth remain an under-appreciated axis of this decline. Researchers deployed AURORA, a generative multi-modality framework combining biological data clocks, to screen for interventions that narrow biological age gaps. The algorithm identified Neisseria flavescens, a common oral commensal, as a top candidate linked to decelerated aging signatures, beneficial gut taxa, and targeted metabolite biosynthesis. When investigators isolated live strains of the bacterium, functional assays demonstrated that colonization extended both lifespan and healthspan in nematodes. Furthermore, supplementing aged mice with heat-killed bacterial preparations shifted their systemic serum metabolome, hepatic gene transcription profiles, and intestinal microbial communities back toward youthful physical baselines.

These findings position the oral microbiome as an active mediator of systemic physiological maintenance rather than a passive bystander. While the computational predictions and cross-species rescue experiments demonstrate robust geroprotective activity in invertebrate and murine models, the current evidentiary boundary stops short of human clinical trials. Specific cohort demographics, longitudinal safety parameters, and the exact biochemical cascades that transmit signals from the oral cavity to internal organs require rigorous clinical validation before any therapeutic applications can be established.

## Key takeaways

- Generative AI modeling identified the oral commensal Neisseria flavescens as a top predictor of decelerated biological aging in humans.
- Live bacterial administration extended lifespan and healthspan parameters in nematode functional assays.
- Supplementation with heat-killed bacterial preparations restored youthful metabolic, transcriptomic, and microbial states in aged mice.
