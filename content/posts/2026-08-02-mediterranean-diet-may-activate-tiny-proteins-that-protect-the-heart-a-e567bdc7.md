A small pilot study finds that older adults who closely follow a Mediterranean eating pattern have higher blood levels of two mitochondrial microproteins tied to cellular health.

## Three things to remember

- Higher adherence to Mediterranean diet linked to more humanin and SHMOOSE.
- Olive oil, fish, legumes tied to humanin; olive oil and fewer refined carbs to SHMOOSE.
- Study is observational; cannot prove diet causes changes.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The Mediterranean diet, rich in olive oil, fish, and legumes, provides polyphenols and omega-3 fatty acids that enhance mitochondrial function. These nutrients may upregulate the expression of mitochondrial microproteins like humanin and SHMOOSE, which protect against oxidative stress and apoptosis. Humanin binds to the insulin-like growth factor receptor and inhibits Bax-mediated cell death, while SHMOOSE interacts with mitochondrial proteins to stabilize the electron transport chain. This aligns with Warburg's emphasis on mitochondrial respiration and Popp's idea of coherent cellular signaling, suggesting that diet-induced microprotein production is a mechanistic link between nutrition and cellular resilience.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional Mediterranean diets, as practiced by ancient Greeks and Romans, emphasized olive oil, fish, legumes, and whole grains—foods that naturally support mitochondrial health. The physical mechanism is that these foods are rich in monounsaturated fats and polyphenols, which reduce mitochondrial reactive oxygen species production and enhance mitophagy. This ancestral pattern mirrors modern findings that such a diet upregulates protective microproteins, offering a evolutionary rationale for why these foods have been staples in longevity-promoting cultures.
