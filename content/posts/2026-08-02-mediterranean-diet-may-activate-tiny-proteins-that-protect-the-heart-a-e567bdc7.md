A small pilot study finds that older adults who closely follow a Mediterranean eating pattern have higher blood levels of two mitochondrial microproteins tied to cellular health.

## Three things to remember

- Higher adherence to Mediterranean diet linked to more humanin and SHMOOSE.
- Olive oil, fish, legumes tied to humanin; olive oil and fewer refined carbs to SHMOOSE.
- Study is observational; cannot prove diet causes changes.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The Mediterranean diet, rich in olive oil, fish, and legumes, provides polyphenols, omega-3 fatty acids, and monounsaturated fats that enhance mitochondrial biogenesis and reduce oxidative stress. These dietary components activate pathways such as SIRT1 and AMPK, which upregulate the transcription of mitochondrial genes, including those encoding humanin and SHMOOSE. The observed increase in these microproteins with diet adherence suggests a direct nutritional modulation of mitochondrial peptide synthesis, supporting Wallace's concept of mitochondrial-nuclear crosstalk and Cohen's framework of MDPs as biomarkers of mitochondrial health. This bridges historical mitochondrial biology with modern nutrigenomics, indicating that diet can fine-tune mitochondrial signaling to promote cellular resilience.
