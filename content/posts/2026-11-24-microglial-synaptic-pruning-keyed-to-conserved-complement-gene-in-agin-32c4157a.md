Cross-species transcriptomics pinpoint a conserved complement gene driving microglial clearance of functional synapses during non-pathological brain aging.

To map the genetic architecture of non-pathological cognitive decline, researchers conducted a cross-species bioinformatic analysis combining bibliometric mapping of 480 studies with transcriptomic datasets from healthy young and aged humans, mice, and rats. The screen isolated 11 shared differentially expressed genes associated with physiological brain aging across all three species. Among these candidates, the complement cascade gene C1QB displayed the highest coding sequence conservation. Functional enrichment analyses revealed that age-related upregulation of C1QB correlates strongly with increased microglial efferocytosis, the enzymatic recognition and engulfment of cellular targets. Rather than restricting debris removal to dying cells, this elevated complement expression tags intact, functional synapses for microglial phagocytosis, triggering excessive synaptic clearance that destroys the physical architecture underlying structural plasticity in aging neural circuits.

These findings expand the molecular framework of non-pathological cognitive aging by establishing a shared transcriptomic link between complement activation and microglial synaptic removal. Most previous models derived from Alzheimer's disease architectures or single-species rodent lines, confounding healthy brain aging with explicit neurodegenerative pathology. Identifying C1QB as an evolutionarily conserved driver provides a target for interventions aimed at preserving synaptic density. However, because this study relies entirely on computational bioinformatic analysis of surrogate transcriptomic datasets, these findings do not establish a direct causal link between C1QB expression, efferocytosis rates, and cognitive decline in living organisms. Furthermore, computational gene expression markers cannot evaluate tissue-specific anti-inflammatory, joint, enzymatic, or mucosal benefits across distinct organ systems.

## Key takeaways

- A cross-species bioinformatic analysis of human, mouse, and rat brains identified 11 shared genes driving physiological brain aging.
- The complement gene C1QB showed the highest evolutionary conservation and correlated strongly with microglial efferocytosis of functional synapses.
- Bibliometric mapping of 480 studies confirmed efferocytosis as an emerging focus alongside classical neuroinflammation and synaptic plasticity pathways.
