A new review explores critical flicker fusion frequency—the point where flickering light looks steady—as a stable trait that may mark the edge of adult brain plasticity.



## Three things to remember

- CFFF is highly stable within individuals, unlike spatial skills.
- It resists general training but may change with specific visual tasks.
- Links to memory or focus remain hypotheses, not proven facts.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The preprint's proposal that CFFF marks a boundary between plastic and non-plastic neural systems aligns with the biophysical concept of coherent signaling. Popp's biophotons and Pollack's EZ water both emphasize the role of electromagnetic and structured water dynamics in cellular communication. CFFF, as a measure of temporal resolution in visual processing, may reflect the efficiency of such coherent signaling in neural networks. If neural plasticity is constrained by the stability of these biophysical processes, then CFFF could serve as a macroscopic indicator of the underlying molecular and quantum-level coherence, linking the review's hypothesis to established biophysical phenomena.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional lifestyles often involve rhythmic exposure to natural light and darkness, which may optimize CFFF and neural plasticity. For example, ancestral patterns of hunting and gathering required acute visual temporal discrimination, potentially maintaining high CFFF. The physical mechanism could involve the entrainment of circadian rhythms and the regulation of neurotransmitters like dopamine, which modulate both visual temporal processing and plasticity. Additionally, practices like meditation or focused attention, common in traditional cultures, might enhance CFFF by promoting neural coherence, as suggested by studies on mindfulness and perceptual thresholds.
