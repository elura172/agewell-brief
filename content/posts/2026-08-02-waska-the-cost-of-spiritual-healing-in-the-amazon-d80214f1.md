Indigenous voices highlight the commodification of sacred plant medicine in the Amazon.
## Three things to remember

- Ayahuasca marketed as shortcut to healing and enlightenment.
- Indigenous storyteller Nina Gualinga calls it commodification and extractivism.
- Cultural appropriation and environmental destruction continue in the Amazon.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The modern report on ayahuasca commodification connects to historical ethnobotanical and anthropological work by exposing the tension between the biochemical extraction of active compounds (like DMT and harmala alkaloids) and the holistic, relational context in which these plants are traditionally used. Schultes' documentation of ritual use and Lévi-Strauss's structural analysis both emphasize that the efficacy of plant medicine is inseparable from the cultural, ecological, and spiritual framework. The current trend of marketing ayahuasca as a 'shortcut' to healing reduces this complex system to a consumable product, thereby severing the mechanistic link between the plant's chemistry and its traditional ceremonial setting, which includes dietary restrictions, musical accompaniment, and community guidance.
