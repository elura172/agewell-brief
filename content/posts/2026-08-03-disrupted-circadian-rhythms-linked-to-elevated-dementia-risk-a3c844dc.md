New research indicates that circadian misalignment may heighten dementia risk, reflecting chronobiology's foundational principle that human physiology is tuned to ancestral light-dark cycles.



## Three things to remember

- Circadian disruption is associated with increased dementia risk in a recent report.
- Potential mechanisms include neuroinflammation and impaired amyloid-beta clearance.
- Maintaining consistent sleep and light exposure supports brain health.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The modern finding that circadian disruption elevates dementia risk is mechanistically bridged to Halberg's and Saper's work through the glymphatic system and neuroinflammation. The glymphatic system, which clears amyloid-beta from the brain, is regulated by the circadian clock and is most active during sleep. Disrupted rhythms impair this clearance, leading to amyloid-beta accumulation and neuroinflammation, as shown in recent studies. Saper's SCN degeneration in Alzheimer's further explains how clock dysfunction accelerates pathology, validating the historical emphasis on the SCN as a master regulator.

**Ancestral parallel:** Ancestral humans lived in strict synchrony with solar light-dark cycles, with no artificial light after dusk. This ensured robust circadian entrainment, optimal sleep, and efficient glymphatic clearance of metabolic waste. Modern artificial lighting and irregular schedules disrupt this ancient rhythm, increasing dementia risk. Traditional practices like morning sunlight exposure and consistent sleep schedules mirror ancestral patterns and support circadian stability, offering a protective lifestyle parallel.
