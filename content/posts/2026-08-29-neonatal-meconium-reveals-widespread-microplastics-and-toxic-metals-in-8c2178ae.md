A biomonitoring study of urban South Asian newborns detects synthetic polymer fragments and heavy metals inside initial meconium samples.

In a cross-sectional biomonitoring study conducted in Dhaka, Bangladesh, researchers analyzed neonatal meconium from thirty Cesarean-delivered infants to map early-life chemical exposures. Using stereomicroscopy, scanning electron microscopy, Raman spectroscopy, and inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry, the team screened the first bowel movements for fifteen distinct metals alongside microplastic occurrence, morphology, and polymer composition. Every analyzable meconium sample contained detectable microplastics, registering a median burden of 149 particles per gram of wet weight. These synthetic particles were overwhelmingly dominated by polyethylene terephthalate fragments and nylon fibers. Simultaneously, all fifteen targeted metals appeared across every analyzable sample, including median concentrations of 1.18 micrograms per gram of lead and 3.92 micrograms per gram of chromium.

The simultaneous recovery of synthetic polymer debris and heavy metals in neonatal waste points to pervasive environmental burdens crossing biological barriers during gestation. Because statistical correlations between the particle counts and metal concentrations dissolved after multiple-testing correction, the physical pathways by which these materials enter and accumulate in fetal tissues likely operate independently or through distinct exposure routes. While maternal breast milk was also sampled from a consenting subset to provide complementary exposure context, the cross-sectional architecture captures only a single snapshot of accumulation near the end of gestation without resolving exact environmental sources or long-term developmental trajectories.

## Key takeaways

- Neonatal meconium from thirty newborns in Dhaka was analyzed for polymer particles and fifteen distinct elemental metals.
- Every analyzable meconium sample contained microplastics, dominated by polyethylene terephthalate fragments and nylon fibers.
- All fifteen measured metals were detected in every analyzable sample, including notable concentrations of lead and chromium.
