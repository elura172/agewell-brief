## Q&A: How aging affects cells' recycling centers

Lysosomes, the cell's recycling hubs, may play a role in age-related diseases like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's.

### Three things to know

- Lysosomes are cell recycling centers that break down harmful molecules.
- Problems with lysosome function are linked to common age-related conditions.
- Scientists are learning more about lysosomes' broader role in health.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The modern finding that lysosomal dysfunction accelerates aging and contributes to Alzheimer's and Parkinson's directly validates de Duve's original concept of lysosomes as cellular recycling hubs. Cuervo's work provides the mechanistic link: with age, lysosomal acidification and enzyme activity decline, impairing autophagy and leading to the buildup of toxic aggregates (e.g., amyloid-beta, alpha-synuclein). This bridges the historical discovery of the organelle's function to contemporary understanding of age-related disease, confirming that lysosomal health is critical for cellular homeostasis and longevity.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional lifestyle practices such as intermittent fasting and caloric restriction have been shown to upregulate autophagy and enhance lysosomal function, mirroring ancestral eating patterns where food scarcity was common. This physical mechanism—activation of cellular recycling pathways during fasting—likely evolved to maintain cellular health during periods of nutrient deprivation, and modern research suggests that mimicking these ancestral conditions can mitigate age-related lysosomal decline and reduce risk of neurodegenerative diseases.
