By embedding a detailed spiking neural network of the cerebellum inside a virtual mouse brain, researchers uncovered how microscopic cellular inhibition dictates large-scale cortical synchronization.

To untangle how microscopic cellular activities dictate large-scale brain dynamics, researchers constructed a multiscale digital brain simulator that nests a spiking neural network of the olivocerebellar microcircuit within a mean-field virtual mouse brain. Bound together by an atlas-based long-range connectome and calibrated against empirical data from primary sensory and motor cortices alongside the cerebellum, the model allowed scientists to systematically sever critical circuit connections in silico. The simulations revealed that Purkinje cell inhibition boosts the computational efficiency of the cerebellar engine by decorrelating the electrical activity of the cerebellar nuclei. Furthermore, tracing these pathways identified the direct route from mossy fibers to the cerebellar nuclei as the specific internal circuit mechanism driving coherence between the primary motor and sensory cortices.

This architecture demonstrates a direct mechanistic bridge connecting the biophysical properties of a localized cerebellar microcircuit to widespread cortical sensorimotor processing. By linking individual cellular inhibition patterns to coordinated large-scale brain rhythms, the framework offers a versatile virtual laboratory for exploring physiological and pathological brain states across multiple spatial scales. However, because these causal interactions were derived entirely from virtual mouse brain simulations rather than direct biological interventions, the exact translation of these in silico circuit dependencies to living mammalian nervous systems remains to be verified.

## Key takeaways

- A novel multiscale digital brain simulator integrates a spiking cerebellar microcircuit into a virtual mouse brain.
- Purkinje cell inhibition increases computational efficiency by decorrelating electrical signals within the cerebellar nuclei.
- The pathway connecting mossy fibers directly to cerebellar nuclei specifically drives motor and sensory cortical coherence.
