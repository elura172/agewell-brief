The agency's health-fraud list now includes pre-workout products, alongside earlier alerts for supplements marketed for sleep, skin, pain, and bodybuilding.

## Three things to remember

- FDA's list flags products with undeclared drug ingredients.
- Recent additions: PRE-FORMANCE BLACK and RAPTURE Preworkout.
- Earlier alerts cover sleep, skin, pain, and bodybuilding products.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The FDA's detection of undeclared drug ingredients in supplements relies on analytical chemistry (e.g., LC-MS) to identify active pharmaceutical ingredients that bypass regulatory approval. These hidden drugs, such as anabolic steroids or sedatives, engage specific molecular targets (e.g., androgen receptors, GABA-A receptors) and alter physiological pathways—protein synthesis, neuromuscular inhibition—consistent with Guyton's principles of dose-response and homeostatic disruption. Kelsey's legacy underpins the regulatory framework that mandates post-market surveillance, validating the modern approach of flagging products with undisclosed pharmacological agents that pose unanticipated toxicity and drug-interaction risks.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional herbal remedies, such as those used in Ayurveda or Traditional Chinese Medicine, were prepared from whole plants with complex phytochemical mixtures, relying on synergistic and often mild effects. In contrast, modern tainted supplements often contain isolated synthetic drugs at high potency, which disrupt the body's homeostatic feedback loops (e.g., thyroid or adrenal axes) in ways that whole-plant preparations rarely do. This highlights a mechanistic divergence: ancestral practices favored gradual modulation of physiological set-points, whereas hidden drug ingredients impose abrupt, supraphysiological stimuli, increasing risk of adverse events.
