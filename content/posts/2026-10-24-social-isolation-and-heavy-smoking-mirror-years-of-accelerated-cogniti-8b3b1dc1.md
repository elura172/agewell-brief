An analysis of 13,866 adults reveals that low social satisfaction and heavy smoking degrade sustained attention by up to nearly six age-equivalent years.

Cognitive control over attention fades gradually across adulthood, yet modifiable lifestyle pressures can drastically accelerate this neural decline. Researchers examined 13,866 participants from the All of Us Research Program, tracking sustained attention using the Gradual-Onset Continuous Performance Task. This task measures perceptual sensitivity, response criteria, reaction times, and temporal variability to generate a composite cognitive efficiency score via principal component analysis. Social satisfaction emerged as the most potent lifestyle predictor in the cohort. Individuals reporting low social satisfaction exhibited a cognitive efficiency deficit equivalent to 5.8 extra years of chronological aging compared to those with high social satisfaction. Smoking tracked a severe dose-dependent trajectory, where individuals smoking more than twenty cigarettes daily suffered a five-year age-equivalent deficit relative to lifetime non-smokers. Furthermore, bootstrapped models demonstrated that combinations of multiple risk factors, especially those anchored by social deprivation, inflicted cognitive deficits far exceeding simple additive calculations, pointing to deeply interactive vulnerabilities within neural networks.

These findings reframe attentional decline not as a uniform, inevitable biological decay, but as a dynamic process shaped by compounding environmental and behavioral pressures. Translating these cross-sectional associations into age-equivalent years bridges statistical modeling with intuitive clinical interpretation, laying bare how lifestyle factors intersect inside human populations. Yet, because this analysis relies on cross-sectional data, it cannot definitively prove that isolation or smoking directly causes the observed drops in perceptual sensitivity and reaction-time stability. Unmeasured confounding variables and potential survival biases, particularly regarding alcohol consumption and cardiovascular metrics, mean these estimates capture associations rather than guaranteed longitudinal trajectories.

## Key takeaways

- Low social satisfaction tracks with an estimated 5.8 additional age-equivalent years of diminished attentional control.
- Smoking more than twenty cigarettes daily yields an approximately five-year cognitive deficit versus never-smokers.
- Co-occurring lifestyle risk factors interact to produce cognitive efficiency deficits larger than standard additive models predict.

### Source

Reported from [Social and behavioral risk factors are associated with age-related differences in sustained attention: A cross-sectional GradCPT analysis of the All of Us dataset](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.01.26364102) (medrxiv).
