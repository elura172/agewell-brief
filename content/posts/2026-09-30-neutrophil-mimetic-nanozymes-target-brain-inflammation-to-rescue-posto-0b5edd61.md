By cloaking catalytic nanoparticles in leukocyte membranes, researchers breached the compromised blood-brain barrier to quench oxidative stress and restore metabolic balance in aged murine microglia.

Surgery and anesthesia frequently trigger a destructive cascade in the aging brain, characterized by oxidative stress, structural breakdown of the blood-brain barrier, and a shift in microglial metabolism that drives persistent neuroinflammation and cognitive decline. To intercept this damage, investigators engineered a biomimetic nanoformulation termed PCN, which combines palladium and hollow ceria nanoparticles encased within isolated neutrophil membranes. Leveraging the natural adhesion proteins of leukocytes, these catalytic carriers home directly to sites of active neuroinflammation and cross the leaky vascular wall. Once inside the brain parenchyma, the nanoparticle core acts as a broad-spectrum scavenger of reactive oxygen species, neutralizing the oxidative radicals that sustain tissue injury.

Beyond direct chemical scavenging, the intervention fundamentally alters immune cell metabolism within the neural microenvironment. Resident microglia in aged, postoperative subjects typically abandon efficient aerobic respiration in favor of glycolysis, locking them into a damaging pro-inflammatory state that destroys synaptic architecture. The nanozyme platform halts this metabolic reprogramming, suppressing glycolytic flux while restoring oxidative phosphorylation to calm microglial activation and preserve synaptic integrity. In aged mice subjected to surgical stress, this molecular containment successfully protected cognitive performance. However, these mechanistic insights derive exclusively from murine models, meaning human translational safety, pharmacokinetic scaling, and clinical efficacy remain entirely unproven.

## Key takeaways

- Neutrophil membrane coatings allow synthetic nanoparticles to exploit native leukocyte adhesion receptors and cross the compromised blood-brain barrier.
- Palladium and ceria cores catalytically scavenge destructive reactive oxygen species to preserve vascular wall integrity in the aging brain.
- Restoring microglial aerobic respiration over runaway glycolysis quenches neuroinflammation and protects vulnerable synaptic connections.
