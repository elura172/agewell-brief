A new analysis by Originality.ai suggests most herbal remedy books on Amazon may be AI-generated, raising concerns about quality and accuracy.
## Three things to remember

- Originality.ai scanned 558 herbal remedy titles on Amazon.
- Found 82% likely written by AI, from January to September.
- Examples include ginkgo tinctures and citrus-immune gummies.

## How to interpret it


### Historical Biophysics & Lineage
The modern analysis by Originality.ai reveals that 82% of current herbal remedy books on Amazon are likely AI-generated, lacking the empirical rigor and botanical accuracy that characterized historical texts like those of Culpeper and Parkinson. This connects to the historical antecedents by highlighting a decline in quality and reliability: while early herbalists relied on direct observation and traditional knowledge passed down through generations, AI-generated content often synthesizes information without verification, potentially propagating errors. The mechanistic bridge lies in the loss of the 'human-in-the-loop' validation process—historical texts were grounded in practical experience and often included warnings about toxicity and dosage, whereas AI models may not adequately incorporate such safety nuances, leading to potential health risks for readers.

**Ancestral parallel:** Traditional herbalism, as practiced by indigenous cultures and ancient civilizations (e.g., Traditional Chinese Medicine, Ayurveda), was based on a deep, empirical understanding of local flora and their effects on the human body, often developed over centuries of trial and error. This knowledge was transmitted orally and through written texts, with a strong emphasis on the holistic context of the individual (e.g., constitution, season, and environment). The modern proliferation of AI-generated herbal books undermines this ancestral wisdom by commodifying and homogenizing knowledge without the cultural and ecological specificity that made traditional remedies effective. The physical mechanism behind this is the 'dose-response' and 'phytochemical synergy'—traditional preparations often used whole plants or specific combinations that balanced active compounds, whereas AI-generated content may oversimplify or misrepresent these complex interactions, potentially leading to ineffective or unsafe usage.
