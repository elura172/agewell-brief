Doubling its cellular scale, the updated human cell atlas maps transcription factors and reveals the diverse, context-dependent nature of cellular senescence.

The Tabula Sapiens 2.0 atlas expands single-cell transcriptomic mapping by incorporating data from nine new donors, doubling the total cell count, and introducing four new tissues. Crucially, the dataset includes multiple organs sampled from four individual donors, establishing a controlled baseline that removes confounding variables from distinct genetic backgrounds, chronological ages, and epigenetic histories. Within this multi-organ architecture, the atlas captures transcripts from nearly every human transcription factor. This comprehensive capture maps putative cell-type specificity across diverse tissues, illuminating how regulatory proteins operate within specialized cellular environments.

Beyond general gene regulation, the transcriptomic data allow researchers to examine cells expressing canonical markers of cellular senescence across different tissue microenvironments. Rather than following a uniform profile, these senescent cells display broad transcriptomic heterogeneity, demonstrating that aging-related growth arrest adapts to local tissue contexts and metabolic demands. While this expansive atlas generates new hypotheses regarding senescence-associated pathways, the current transcriptomic architecture cannot yet establish the exact biochemical mechanisms driving these cell-state transitions or confirm their causal roles in systemic aging.

## Key takeaways

- The updated atlas doubles previous cell counts and adds four new tissues from nine additional human donors.
- Tissue sampling from four shared donors controls for genetic background, chronological age, and epigenetic variation.
- Profiling cells expressing senescence genes exposes profound transcriptomic heterogeneity across different human tissues.
