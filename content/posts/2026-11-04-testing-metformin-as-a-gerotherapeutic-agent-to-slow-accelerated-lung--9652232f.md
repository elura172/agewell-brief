A new phase III clinical trial investigates whether repurposing metformin can halt cellular senescence and mitochondrial dysfunction to slow pulmonary function decline in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease stubbornly resists structural modification, manifesting less as a localized airway injury and more as a systemic collapse of biological time. Patients suffer from accelerated lung aging marked by relentless emphysema progression, oxidative stress, and the accumulation of senescent cells that secrete inflammatory signals into surrounding tissue. Conventional therapies suppress symptoms and manage acute exacerbations, but they fail to arrest the underlying cellular decay. To confront this root mechanism, the AMICO trial deploys a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III design to test metformin as a gerotherapeutic intervention. Researchers are tracking 212 stable patients with centrilobular emphysema and rapid forced expiratory volume decline, administering 850 milligrams of metformin twice daily for 36 months alongside standard care. The drug targets the intracellular machinery driving oxidative damage and mitochondrial failure, aiming to physically stabilize lung architecture before irreversible elastic recoil is lost.

By shifting the therapeutic target from smooth muscle relaxation to the biophysical hallmarks of aging, the trial protocol operationalizes a fundamental geroscience hypothesis. The primary endpoint measures the annual rate of forced expiratory volume decline over three years, complemented by secondary assessments of exercise capacity, muscle strength, emphysema progression, and systemic biomarkers of aging. Yet, because the study is currently evaluating a trial design rather than reporting completed outcomes, its ultimate impact on long-term clinical trajectories remains unproven. The evidentiary boundary rests on whether systemic administration of an established metabolic modulator can sufficiently alter the pulmonary microenvironment to intercept biological aging in human lung tissue.

## Key takeaways

- The AMICO trial randomizes 212 stable chronic obstructive pulmonary disease patients to receive metformin or placebo for 36 months.
- Cellular mechanisms targeted by the trial include oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, and cellular senescence.
- Primary and secondary endpoints track forced expiratory volume decline, emphysema progression, functional exercise capacity, and aging biomarkers.
