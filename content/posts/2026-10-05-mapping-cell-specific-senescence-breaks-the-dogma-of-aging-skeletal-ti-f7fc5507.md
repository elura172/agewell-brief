Treating p16 and p21 as distinct cellular states reveals how distinct skeletal niches drive bone aging.

Bone is far from a static mineral scaffold; it functions as an immune-active organ sustained by continuous dialogue among skeletal, hematopoietic, immune, adipose, vascular, and neural compartments. As the organism ages, this cellular network destabilizes due to stable cell-cycle arrest, metabolic remodeling, and a heterogeneous senescence-associated secretory phenotype that alters the surrounding tissue architecture. Traditional frameworks attribute skeletal decline primarily to hormonal shifts or broad, low-grade inflammation, but these generalized models miss the spatial and temporal complexity of the aging skeleton. By treating p16- and p21-associated senescence states as functionally distinct, researchers can map how damage accumulates differently across bone marrow stromal cells, osteoblast lineages, osteocytes, and immune populations housed within a marrow ecosystem marked by vascular and neural decay.

This refined taxonomy of cellular aging connects core molecular defects to diverse pathologies including osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, periodontitis, and delayed fracture healing. Moving beyond blunt senolytic clearance requires precision strategies that leverage cell identity, anatomical location, and disease stage to guide emerging interventions like senomorphics and immune-mediated clearance. Yet, because this synthesis integrates disparate compartments and disease models into an overarching framework, it remains a conceptual roadmap that still demands validation through targeted, biomarker-guided clinical testing before these precision windows can be translated into human therapeutics.

## Key takeaways

- Cellular senescence disrupts the bone marrow ecosystem through stable cell-cycle arrest and distinct secretory phenotypes.
- Distinguishing p16- from p21-associated states enables precise therapeutic targeting based on cell identity and anatomical location.
- Targeted interventions span senolytics, senomorphics, and immune-mediated clearance mapped across multiple degenerative bone diseases.
