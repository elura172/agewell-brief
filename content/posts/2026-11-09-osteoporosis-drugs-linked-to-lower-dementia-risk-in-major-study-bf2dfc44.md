A comprehensive population study of 120,000 patients reveals that common nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates are associated with a significant reduction in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias.

## The biophysical intersection of bone and brain health

Skeletal integrity and central nervous system health are linked through shared systemic environment dynamics, microvascular flow, and inflammatory regulation. Osteoporosis and dementia frequently co-occur in aging adults, bound by overlapping risk factors such as advanced age, female sex, and diminished physical activity. When bone remodeling loses its homeostatic balance, the biological stress is rarely isolated to the skeleton alone.

The biophysical model of physiology demonstrates that chronic systemic stress drains systemic reserves, accelerating cellular breakdown across multiple organ systems. The work of Hans Selye showed how allostatic load affects the entire body. In the neurovascular unit and the skeletal matrix, persistent inflammatory signals accelerate biological decay. Identifying existing therapies that preserve bone mass while modulating systemic inflammation offers a unique strategy for protecting cognitive resilience.

## Observational evidence from 120,000 patient records

To explore this physiological connection, researchers at the Department of Pharmacology and Pharmacy at HKUMed analyzed anonymized electronic health records from over 120,000 patients across Hong Kong. All individuals in the cohort were aged 60 or older and had been diagnosed with osteoporosis or experienced fragility fractures—breaks resulting from low-impact stress on weakened bone tissue.

The longitudinal data spanned fifteen years, tracking patient outcomes from 2005 through 2020. The researchers compared patients prescribed nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates, such as alendronate and zoledronate, against untreated individuals and those receiving non-NBP bone therapies. Patients taking NBPs exhibited a 16% lower risk of developing Alzheimer's disease and related dementias compared to untreated controls. When compared directly to patients receiving other classes of osteoporosis drugs, NBP users demonstrated a 24% lower dementia risk.

The observational analysis revealed that the statistical association was especially pronounced among female participants and patients who had previously suffered hip fractures. Based on these risk ratios, the research team calculated that treating 48 osteoporosis patients with NBPs over a five-year period could potentially prevent one incident case of dementia.

## Mechanistic insights and epidemiological interpretation

Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates act primarily by inhibiting key enzymes within the mevalonate pathway inside bone-resorbing osteoclasts, preventing structural breakdown of the bone matrix. Historical insights from Herbert Fleisch demonstrated how these inorganic pyrophosphate analogs bind to hydroxyapatite crystals. Beyond their primary target in bone tissue, this pathway modulation downregulates downstream signaling molecules that drive systemic pro-inflammatory cytokine production.

This enzymatic inhibition is highly relevant to neurodegenerative pathology. Decades of microglial research, pioneered by scientists like Patrick L. McGeer, established that chronic neuroinflammation driven by overactive immune cells is a core driver of Alzheimer's progression. By modulating systemic inflammatory responses and cellular turnover, NBPs may help shield the microvascular network of the brain from inflammatory degradation, preserving structural and functional integrity.

However, scientific rigor requires separating correlation from definitive causation. Because this investigation relied on observational electronic medical records from a single geographic population, it cannot prove that bisphosphonates directly cause a reduction in dementia incidence. Unmeasured lifestyle factors, baseline health differences, or prescribing biases could influence the observed outcomes.

## Clinical implications for healthspan and future research

Dementia currently affects over 55 million individuals globally, a figure projected to reach 139 million by 2050. While direct targeted disease-modifying therapies for Alzheimer's are emerging, challenges regarding accessibility, candidate selection, cost, and risk profiles persist. Identifying safe, widely available, and cost-effective medications already approved for skeletal preservation presents an attractive strategy for reducing neurodegenerative risk.

Lead researchers emphasize that these findings provide a strong empirical rationalization for further clinical trials. Repurposing proven pharmaceuticals with established safety profiles circumvents the multi-decade timelines required for novel drug development. If prospective randomized trials confirm a direct neuroprotective mechanism, NBP therapy could serve a dual role in strengthening bones and safeguarding cognitive function in high-risk populations.

For sovereign adults navigating age-related healthcare choices, these findings highlight the deep systemic interconnectedness of the human body. Maintaining skeletal health through appropriate clinical guidance, physical movement, and targeted metabolic care may yield protective cascades that extend directly into the central nervous system.
