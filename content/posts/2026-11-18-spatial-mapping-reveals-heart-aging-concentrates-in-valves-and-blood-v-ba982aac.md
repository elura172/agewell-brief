Single-cell resolution reveals that age-related inflammation and scarring target the organ's structural plumbing rather than its main muscle.

Most cardiac longevity research focuses on the left ventricle, the powerful muscular pump that drives oxygenated blood into systemic circulation. However, scientists at The Jackson Laboratory used single-cell spatial transcriptomics in mice to map how different heart regions age. Instead of finding uniform decay across the myocardium, the team discovered that signs of age-related inflammation and tissue scarring cluster intensely around the aortic valves and aortic root. In these structural zones, immune cells called macrophages step up the activity of genes that drive chronic inflammation and structural remodeling. Simultaneously, nearby fibroblasts display transcriptomic signatures of fibrosis, laying down excess collagen. Crucially, canonical senescence markers like p21 and inflammatory signals like Ccl8 concentrated in these non-muscular support tissues, revealing that cardiac aging originates primarily within the organ's structural framework.

These findings redefine the mechanical architecture of heart decline by shifting focus from the main engine to the surrounding structural scaffolding. When comparing their rodent spatial maps against existing human heart datasets, the researchers confirmed that identical stress-response and inflammatory pathways activate in aging human cardiac tissue. The team also observed distinct molecular divergence between sexes: aging male fibroblasts favored fibrotic scarring pathways, whereas female fibroblasts altered networks tied to protein production and heart development. However, these transcriptomic maps reflect shifts in cellular gene activity rather than measured changes in organ function or clinical disease. Because the primary cellular mapping relied on mice living in controlled laboratory environments, further functional testing is necessary to determine how these localized inflammatory hotspots alter long-term cardiac output.

## Key takeaways

- Inflammation and fibrotic scarring concentrate primarily around the heart valves and aortic root rather than the myocardium.
- Macrophage and fibroblast crosstalk shifts with age, increasing local inflammatory signaling through Ccl8 and p21 expression.
- Male and female hearts exhibit distinct molecular signatures, with male fibroblasts showing stronger pathways tied to tissue scarring.
