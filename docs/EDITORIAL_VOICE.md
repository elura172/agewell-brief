# Agewell editorial voice

Agewell sounds clear, spirited, respectful, and concrete. It treats readers as capable adults and makes health research interesting without turning uncertainty into theatre.

## The editing order

1. Build the evidence record from the source and, when available, the primary study.
2. Draft the story without adding facts, quotations, scenes, or colour.
3. Edit for publication voice and natural rhythm.
4. Check every material claim, number, name, and limitation against the evidence again.

Style never outranks factual nuance.

## Soft voice rules

- Prefer specific nouns and direct verbs to abstract claims about importance.
- Vary sentence length when the material supports it.
- Remove promotional phrasing, vague attribution, filler, and manufactured punchlines.
- Avoid stock conclusions that merely say more research is needed or the future looks promising. Name the actual next question when it matters.
- Use sentence case for headings.
- Keep medical limits plain and proportional. Do not pile on disclaimers or drain the life from a well-supported sentence.
- Preserve useful house structures, including the three takeaways, when they help the reader.
- Let individual stories breathe. These are preferences, not a template that every article must visibly obey.

This approach is informed in part by [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer), adapted for source-led health publishing rather than applied as an automatic rewrite checklist.
